\chapter{Methods}
\label{chap:methods}

Use the opening paragraph to connect the study design to the research
question. The chapter should give another researcher enough information to
assess the evidence and repeat the work where applicable. Let the length
follow the number of decisions that materially affect the findings.

\section{Data and analysis}
Open this section by telling the reader what evidence you used, which
research question it addresses, and how you turned it into findings. A
page or two is a starting point for a straightforward study. The
subsections below cover how you prepared the evidence and how you analysed
it. Give those stages separate headings when each needs several paragraphs.
A brief account can use two consecutive paragraphs under this section.

\subsection{Data preparation}
A few paragraphs are usually enough. Describe the source and unit of
observation, the exclusions and their rationale, and any transformations
that affect the results. Give counts before and after filtering where they
help the reader judge how much evidence remains. If missing observations
could affect the findings, flag that here before going into detail.

\subsubsection{Missing observations}
Avoid subsubsections by default. A sentence or short paragraph under Data
preparation is usually enough to report how many observations were missing
and how you handled them. Keep a separate heading only when missingness
could materially change the conclusion and the reader needs to find several
paragraphs on it. In that case, explain where the gaps arose, justify your
approach, and report whether another reasonable approach changes the result.

\gerd{Explain whether missing observations changed the conclusions.}
\stefan{Report how many observations were missing.}

\subsection{Analysis procedure}
In a few paragraphs, connect each analysis to the question it answers. Name
the measure, model, or coding procedure, and describe the assumptions and
checks that affect interpretation. Include enough detail for a reader to
reproduce the main analysis from the same inputs.

\section{User study}
If the thesis includes a user study, start with 1--2 pages on who took part,
how they were recruited, what they did, and what you measured. Give the order
of tasks and any conditions or comparisons needed to interpret the results.
Report relevant consent and ethics procedures. An appendix can hold the full
study materials.
